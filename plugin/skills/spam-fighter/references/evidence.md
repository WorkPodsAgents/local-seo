# What counts as evidence

Google's "Guidelines for representing your business on Google" are the rulebook. A report
works when it shows a listing breaks a named rule, with proof a reviewer can check in minutes.

## Signals and their proof

- **Keyword-stuffed name.** The name on Maps adds services or a place ("Smith Plumbing -
  Emergency Plumber Nyack") that the business's real-world name does not have. Proof: the
  name on its own website, logo, signage photos, or the state business register.
- **Fake or ineligible address.** A mailbox store, virtual office, co-working desk the
  business does not staff, or a house with no business. Proof: the address listed by the
  virtual office provider, Street View, other businesses at the same suite number.
- **Duplicates.** Several listings for one business (same phone, website or owner) to fill
  more of the map. Proof: the shared phone or website from each listing.
- **Lead-generation fronts.** A listing for a trade with no real business behind it, often
  with a call-tracking number and a generic site serving many towns. Proof: the same site or
  number on listings in many cities; no licence for the trade in the state register.
- **Service-area business showing an address.** Only a fact to note, not spam by itself.

## Filing

- **Suggest an edit** on the listing in Maps for one clear fix, such as the name. Edits from
  accounts with a history of accepted edits carry more weight.
- **Business Redressal Complaint Form** (in Google Business Profile Help) for patterns,
  several listings, or when edits were rejected. One form per pattern, the evidence pack
  attached, every claim tied to a link or screenshot.
- Keep a dated log of what was filed and check back after two to four weeks.

Never file on suspicion alone, and never report a competitor only for outranking the user.
